# Strategy S7 — Strengthen EEES78 in the range that matters

**Route:** Do not attack the key lemma or the reduction; attack the *input*.
EEES78 gives $v > C(n,i)^{1/2}$ (outside 12 exceptions), and $1/2$ is exactly
the exponent that closes $j \le 3i/2$ and nothing more. But $1/2$ is a
worst-case exponent forced by small pairs. In the frontier range
$n \in [2j, N(i,j)]$ with $j = ci$, $c > 3/2$, the data below show
$\theta_u(n,i) := \log u / \log C(n,i)$ is typically $0.15$–$0.25$ and at
worst $\approx 0.36$ once $i \ge 30$ — far below $1/2$. If a bound
$v \ge C(n,i)^{\varphi}$ with $\varphi > \theta_{\mathrm{req}}(c)$ can be
proved for $i \ge i_0$ in the frontier window, the *same* key-lemma argument
closes all $j \le ci$ for $i \ge i_0$, leaving finitely many pairs
($i < i_0$) to the sweep. **Verdict up front: the data support this for
$c = 1.75$ comfortably and $c = 2.0$ plausibly; $c = 2.25$ is borderline;
$c = 2.5$ is not supported at moderate $i$. The route does not die — its
breaking point is not where the brief feared (see §6).**

Notation: $C(n,i) = uv$, $u$ on primes $< i$, $v = V_i(n)$ on primes $\ge i$;
$\theta_u = \log u/\log C(n,i)$, so $v = C(n,i)^{1-\theta_u}$ and EEES78 is
$\theta_u < 1/2$ outside the 12 exceptions. Write $\alpha = n/i$.

## 1. What EEES78 actually does (verified text vs reconstruction)

**Verified** (paper abstract, PHASE0 Item 4; plan of attack, EEES78
pp. 258–259, read from a scan of the paper; Shorey–Tijdeman, *Prime factors
of arithmetic progressions and binomial coefficients*, §0.2 and §1.4):

- The theorem is proved by splitting the $(k, c)$-plane, $n = ck$, into
  **five regions**. Region I ($k$ and $c$ both large): show $C > u^2$ "by
  comparing the binomial coefficient with the square of a *simple
  overestimate* for the product of its small prime factors." Region II
  ($k$ large, $c$ small): the symmetric argument with an *underestimate*
  for the product of the large prime factors. Region III ($k$ small, $c$
  large): "careful overestimate of $u$" against underestimate of $v$.
  Region IV ($k, c$ both small): direct computer check, $k \le 494$.
  Region V (leftover cases, certain $k \le 24$): eliminated by "more
  sensitive systematic estimates of the size of $u$."
- The "simple overestimate" is the Erdős-circle bound: if $p^a \| C(n,k)$
  then $p^a \le n$ (Erdős's lemma, from his 1934 Sylvester–Schur proof),
  hence $u \le n^{\pi(k)}$. Shorey–Tijdeman §0.2 quote the block version:
  the product of the small-prime factors of a block of $k$ consecutive
  integers is at most $k!\,(x+k-1)^{\pi(k)}$; for a binomial coefficient the
  $k!$ is divided out.
- EEES78 frame the result against Mahler's theorem (the $k$-smooth part of
  $C(n,k)$ is $< n^{1+\varepsilon}$ for $n$ large relative to $k$,
  ineffectively), and note a near miss in the *other* ($U/V$) convention
  with $V/U < 1.06$ (the pair is garbled in the scan's OCR; flagged, not
  relied on).

**Reconstruction** (standard for this circle, *not* verified line-by-line;
the scan's later pages were not accessible): inside Regions I–III the
$p^a \le n$ bound is refined range by range — primes are split at scales
like $\sqrt{k}$, $k/2$, and the prime-power contribution in each range is
bounded by a mix of $p^a \le n$, Legendre exponent counts, and explicit
$\pi(x)$/$\theta(x)$ estimates; Region II additionally lower-bounds $v$ by
primes in the top block $(n-k, n]$. Nothing in §§3–5 depends on the
reconstructed details; §5 rebuilds the needed estimates from two lemmas I
state and verify exactly.

Two structural observations drive everything below:

1. **EEES78 only ever needed $\theta_u < 1/2$, uniformly in $\alpha \ge 2$.**
   The crude bound $u \le n^{\pi(i)}$ already gives, asymptotically,
   $\theta_u \le 1/[\alpha\log\alpha - (\alpha-1)\log(\alpha-1)]$, which is
   $< 1/2$ iff $\alpha \gtrsim 3.4$. So the *hard* part of EEES78 is small
   $\alpha$ — exactly the part #699 does not need, because the frontier for
   $j = ci$ has $\alpha \ge 2c \ge 3.2$ for $c \ge 1.6$.
2. **The exceptions and near-exceptions are a small-$n$ phenomenon**
   (quantified in §2). The exponent $1/2$ is not approached anywhere in the
   frontier range at moderate $i$.

## 2. The empirical $\theta_u$ study

**Method (exact).** Exhaustive over all $1 \le i \le n/2$, $n \le 2000$
($\approx 10^6$ pairs). $\log u$ is computed by an incremental sweep in $i$
for fixed $n$: factor $n-i$ and $i+1$ by an spf sieve, update
$\log u$ by the prime factors $< i+1$, and add the newly-entering prime
$p = i$ (when prime) with its exact Legendre exponent; $\log C(n,i)$ from
`lgamma`. Script: `S7_theta_sweep.py` (same directory). **Validation:** for
all pairs with $n \le 300$ the incremental values agree with brute-force
factorisation of $C(n,i)$ to $<10^{-9}$ (0 mismatches); and over the full
range the set $\{\theta_u > 1/2\}$ comes out to be *exactly* the 12 EEES78
exception pairs — an independent reproduction of the published list.

## 3. The required strengthening: $\theta_{\mathrm{req}}(c)$

Suppose $v \ge C(n,i)^{\varphi}$ for all $(n,i)$ in the frontier window of
$(i,j)$, and $(n,i,j)$ is a counterexample. The key lemma gives
$v \mid C(j, j-i)$, so $v \le C(j, j-i)$; since $n \ge 2j$,
$C(n,i) \ge C(2j, i)$. A contradiction follows if
$C(2j,i)^{\varphi} > C(j, j-i)$, i.e.

$$\varphi > \varphi_{\mathrm{req}}(i,j) := \frac{\log C(j,\, j-i)}{\log C(2j,\, i)}.$$

(The Vandermonde route of Phase 0 gives the condition
$\varphi > \tfrac12 \log C(2j, 2(j-i))/\log C(2j,i)$; the two thresholds are
*identical asymptotically*, since
$\tfrac12 \cdot 2j\, H\!\left(\tfrac{j-i}{j}\right) = j\, H\!\left(\tfrac{j-i}{j}\right)$,
and differ only in lower-order terms. I use the direct form.)

With $j = ci$ and binary entropy $H$ (nats),
$\log C(j, j-i) \sim ci\, H(1 - 1/c)$ and
$\log C(2j, i) \sim 2ci\, H(1/(2c))$, so

$$\theta_{\mathrm{req}}(c) = \frac{H(1 - 1/c)}{2\,H(1/(2c))}.$$

| $c$ | 1.5 | 1.55 | 1.6 | 1.7 | 1.75 | 1.8 | 1.9 | 2.0 | 2.1 | 2.25 | 2.5 | 3.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\theta_{\mathrm{req}}(c)$ | .5000 | .5172 | .5326 | .5592 | .5707 | .5813 | .6001 | .6163 | .6304 | .6484 | .6725 | .7064 |
| budget $1-\theta_{\mathrm{req}}$ for $\theta_u$ | .5000 | .4828 | .4674 | .4408 | .4293 | .4187 | .3999 | .3837 | .3696 | .3516 | .3275 | .2936 |

Finite-$i$ thresholds approach from below (easier): for $c = 2$,
$\varphi_{\mathrm{req}} = 0.6006,\ 0.6086,\ 0.6119,\ 0.6152,\ 0.6160$ at
$i = 20,\ 50,\ 100,\ 500,\ 2000$. Also note the window endpoint:
$N(i, ci)/i \to 3.56$ ($c=1.6$), $4.52$ ($1.75$), $6.38$ ($2$), $11.1$
($2.5$) — computed exactly at $i = 50, 100, 200$; the strengthening is only
needed for $\alpha \in [2c, N/i]$, and only there.

**Comparison.** Against §2's frontier table: at $c = 1.75$ the empirical sup
($i \ge 50$) is $0.3250$ vs budget $0.4293$ — slack $0.104$. At $c = 2$:
$0.3396$ ($i\ge50$), $0.2918$ ($i\ge100$) vs budget $0.3837$ — slack
$0.044$–$0.092$. At $c = 2.5$: sup *exceeds* budget at $i \ge 30, 50$ and
only clears at $i \ge 100$. So a strengthening of the shape below is
empirically true for $c \le 2$ with room to spare, and the room grows
with $i$.

## 4. The exact conjectural lemma

**Conjectural Lemma S7($c$, $\delta$).** *There exist $i_0$ and $\delta > 0$
such that for all $i \ge i_0$ and all $n$ with $2ci \le n \le N(i, \lfloor ci \rfloor)$,*
$$v \;=\; V_i(n) \;\ge\; C(n,i)^{\,\theta_{\mathrm{req}}(c) + \delta},
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\theta_u(n,i) \;\le\; 1 - \theta_{\mathrm{req}}(c) - \delta.$$

Instantiated targets, in decreasing ambition:

- **S7(1.75):** $\theta_u \le 0.41$ for $i \ge i_0$, $\alpha \in [3.5, 4.6]$.
  Empirical sup at $i \ge 50$ is $0.3250$. Would close $j \le 1.75i$ for
  $i \ge i_0$.
- **S7(2.0):** $\theta_u \le 0.36$ for $i \ge i_0$, $\alpha \in [4, 6.4]$.
  Empirical sup at $i \ge 50$ is $0.3396$; at $i \ge 100$, $0.2918$. Would
  close $j \le 2i$ for $i \ge i_0$ — doubling the proved region's reach in
  ratio terms, and (since $N(i,j)$ grows with $j/i$) clearing a large new
  class of pairs outright.
- **S7(2.5):** $\theta_u \le 0.31$, $\alpha \in [5, 11.1]$. Supported only
  for $i \ge 100$ empirically; not proposed as a first target.

Any S7($c$) + key lemma + EEES78 (for $n > N(i,j)$) + the existing sweep
technology (for $i < i_0$, and EEES78 exceptions) proves #699 for all
$j \le ci$, all $i$.

## 5. Provability: where EEES78's $u$-bound has slack, and the improved bound

EEES78's Region-I overestimate is $u \le n^{\pi(i)}$ (every $p < i$
contributes up to $n$). Asymptotically this gives
$\theta_u \le 1/[\alpha\log\alpha - (\alpha-1)\log(\alpha-1)]$: at the
frontier's low-$\alpha$ edge this is $0.503$ ($c = 1.6$, $\alpha = 3.2$),
$0.445$ ($c = 2$, $\alpha = 4$), $0.395$ ($c = 2.5$, $\alpha = 5$) — in each
case $\approx 0.04$–$0.07$ *above* the budget. So the crude bound never
suffices, but the shortfall is small and localised: it comes almost
entirely from the primes just below $i$, which the crude bound charges a
full $\log n$ each although most of them do not divide $C(n,i)$ at all.
Two exact lemmas capture the saving.

**Lemma A (Erdős; verified).** If $p^a \| C(n,i)$ then $p^a \le n$.
Consequently $p > \sqrt n \Rightarrow e_p \le 1$.
*(Verified computationally: 0 violations over all pairs $n \le 300$,
`S7_lemma_checks.py`; this is the lemma of Erdős's 1934 proof.)*

**Lemma B (near-$i$ characterisation; verified).** Let $p$ be prime with
$i/2 < p < i$ and $i > 4$. Then $v_p(i!) = 1$, and
$e_p(C(n,i)) \ge 1$ if and only if the block $(n-i, n]$ contains **two**
multiples of $p$, or one multiple of $p^2$. In the two-multiples case,
writing $q = \lfloor n/p \rfloor$, the condition is
$p > (n-i)/(q-1)$ with $\alpha i/(q+1) < p \le \alpha i/q$.
*(Verified computationally: 0 mismatches over 130,281 triples
$(n,i,p)$, $n \le 300$.)*

The point: for $p \in (i/2, i)$ the crude bound charges $\log n \approx
\log i$ for *every* such prime ($\theta(i) - \theta(i/2) \approx i/2$ in
total even after using $e_p \le 1$), but Lemma B says $e_p \ge 1$ only for
$p$ lying in explicit short intervals (one per $q$). At $\alpha = 4$ those
intervals have total length $\approx 0.188\,i$ (namely, in units of $i$:
$q=7$: $(0.500, 0.571]$, $q=6$: $(0.600, 0.667]$, $q=5$: $(0.750, 0.800]$,
$q=4$: empty), so the whole range $p \in (i/2, i)$ contributes
$\approx 0.19\,i$ to $\log u$ instead of $\approx 0.5\,i$ — a saving of
$\approx 0.31\,i$, i.e. $\approx 0.14$ in $\theta_u$ at $\alpha = 4$, against
a shortfall of only $0.06$. The same computation at $\alpha = 3.2$ and
$\alpha = 5$ gives total interval length $\approx 0.19\,i$ in both cases.

**The improved $u$-bound to attempt** (target: S7(2.0), so
$\alpha \in [4, 6.4]$, budget $\log u \le 0.36 \log C(n,i) \approx 0.81\,i$
at the worst edge $\alpha = 4$, where $\log C \approx 2.249\,i$):

- **R1, $p \le \sqrt i$:** Lemma A; contribution
  $\le \pi(\sqrt i)\log n = o(i)$.
- **R2, $\sqrt i < p \le i/2$:** Lemma A alone; contribution
  $\le \pi(i/2)\log n = \tfrac12 i\,(1 + o(1))$.
- **R3, $i/2 < p < i$:** Lemma B; contribution $\le$ sum of $\log p$ over
  the Lemma-B intervals (plus a $p^2$-multiple error term, $O(\sqrt n)$ in
  count, negligible), $\approx 0.19\,i$ at $\alpha = 4$, to be bounded
  rigorously with explicit $\pi$-in-interval estimates (Dusart-type).

Total: $\log u \le (0.5 + 0.19 + o(1))\,i \approx 0.69\,i \le 0.81\,i$. ✓
The margin is $\approx 0.12\,i$ in $\log u$ ($\approx 0.05$ in $\theta_u$)
at $c = 2$; at $c = 1.75$ it is $\approx 0.2\,i$; at $c = 2.5$
($\alpha = 5$ edge: budget $0.31 \cdot 2.502\,i = 0.78\,i$ vs assembled
$0.69\,i$) the margin thins to $\approx 0.09\,i$ *before* accounting for
the $o(1)$ losses, and the Lemma-B intervals get shorter (down to
$0.033\,i$), where explicit prime-gap/$\pi$ estimates are weakest. That —
not the truth of the bound — is what makes $c = 2.5$ unattractive.

Honest obstacles: (i) R2's $\tfrac12 i$ is a leading-term estimate;
converting $\pi(i/2)\log n$ and the R3 interval counts into explicit
inequalities valid for $i \ge i_0$ will push $i_0$ up (plausibly into the
hundreds or low thousands — acceptable, since the sweep already clears
$i \le 1000$ pairs with $N(i,j) \le 10000$, but the $i < i_0$ residue for
$j \le ci$ must be re-examined: pairs with $N(i,j) > 10000$ and
$i < i_0$ are *not* currently cleared, so S7 must be paired with a
targeted finite check there). (ii) Lemma B's $p^2$ case and $e_p \ge 2$
subcases need exact handling (they are rare: $e_p \ge 2$ for
$p \in (i/2, i)$ forces a multiple of $p^2$ in the block or two multiples
one of which is a multiple of $p^2$... precisely: $e_p = \sum_{\text{block}}
v_p - 1 \ge 2$ requires $\sum v_p \ge 3$). (iii) Nothing here touches
$i$ small or $\alpha < 3$; those stay with EEES78 + sweep, as now.

## 6. Breaking point, and honest verdict

**Where $\theta_u$ is actually largest.** Globally: the 12 EEES78
exceptions (max $0.648$ at $(10,5)$), then a cliff — the largest
non-exception value anywhere in $n \le 2000$ is $0.49472$ at $(32,13)$,
and every value $> 0.44$ has $n \le 81$. In the frontier windows the worst
points are $(378, 61)$ ($\theta_u = 0.3396$, entering the $c = 2$ and
$c = 2.5$ windows), $(392, 109)$ ($0.3250$), $(156, 47)$ ($0.4044$,
$c = 1.6$ window at $i = 47$), $(68, 21)$ ($0.4176$, $i = 21$). The sup
over the frontier window is **monotone-decreasing in the $i$-cutoff** at
every $c$ tested, and the band maxima decay with $i$ (e.g. $\alpha \in
[4,5)$: $0.314 \to 0.291 \to 0.261 \to 0.240$ across
$i \in [50,100), [100,200), [200,400), [400,700)$). The supremum does
**not** approach $1/2$ in the relevant range; it approaches a limit
$\lesssim 0.30$. The strengthening is therefore *not* false, and the route
does not die at the feared breaking point.

The genuine danger zone is elsewhere: the line $\alpha = 2$ ($n = 2i$),
where $\theta_u$ fluctuates ($0.228$–$0.360$ at sampled $i = 50$–$999$)
with no visible decay, and $\alpha \in [2, 3)$ generally (band sup $0.4245$
at $(222, 109)$ for $i \ge 150$). But $\alpha < 3$ frontier points would
require $j < 1.5i$ — already closed. A strengthening attempt must be
stated *only* for the frontier window $\alpha \ge 2c$, as in §4; stated
uniformly over $\alpha \ge 2$ it would still be true empirically
($\theta_u \le 0.43$ for $i \ge 150$) but useless, since the budget at
$c \to 1.5$ is $0.5$ and EEES78 already owns that edge.

**Verdict.**

- **Data support: yes.** For every $c \le 2$, empirically
  $\theta_u \le 1 - \theta_{\mathrm{req}}(c) - \delta$ holds on the whole
  frontier window once $i \ge 50$ (with $\delta \approx 0.04$ at $c = 2$,
  $\approx 0.10$ at $c = 1.75$), and the margin grows with $i$.
- **Recommended target: S7(2.0)** — $v \ge C(n,i)^{0.64}$ in the window
  $4i \le n \le N(i, 2i)$, $i \ge i_0$ — via the R1/R2/R3 bound of §5,
  whose key new ingredient is Lemma B (exact, verified). S7(1.75) is the
  fallback with roughly double the margin.
- **Not recommended:** $c = 2.5$ as a first target (empirically false at
  $i \in [30, 100)$ on the window — sup $0.3396$ vs budget $0.3275$ — and
  the §5 assembled bound's margin there is within explicit-estimate
  noise); $c = 2.25$ only after S7(2.0) succeeds.
- **What this route cannot do:** close $c$ beyond $\approx 2.5$ (budgets
  shrink like $1 - \theta_{\mathrm{req}}(c)$ while $L(\alpha)$ does not),
  and it says nothing about $j > ci$ for any fixed $c$ — it enlarges the
  proved region, it does not finish the problem. Its output should be
  reported exactly that way.
